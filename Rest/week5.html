WEEK 05

Rest in God Alone

Learning from Psalm 62

What does it actually mean for our souls to find rest in God alone?

KEY SCRIPTURE

Psalm 62

"Yes, my soul, find rest in God."

Psalm 62:5

OPENING QUESTION

What Is Rest?

Psalm 62 opens with the idea that the soul finds rest in God alone.

What do you think rest means here? Is it physical, emotional, spiritual, or something else?

DISCUSSION

Psalm 62 Questions

  1. What verses or images in this psalm feel calming to you? Which feel challenging?
  2. The psalm repeats the idea of God alone. What are the other things we're tempted to rest in instead?
  3. How does resting in God differ from "giving up" or becoming passive?
  4. In verses 3–4, David describes being attacked or shaken. What does it look like to rest while life feels unstable?
  5. Verse 5 says, "Yes, my soul, find rest in God." Why do you think David has to tell his soul to rest?
  6. Where in your life do you feel pressure to prove, fix, or control something instead of resting?
  7. How might trust be an active form of rest rather than a lazy one?
  8. Verses 9–10 warn against resting in status, wealth, or power. Why do those things feel so restful at first?
  9. How can even good things—work, relationships, ministry, school—become places of false rest?
  10. What would it look like to "pour out your heart" to God as a form of rest?
  11. How could this psalm reshape the way you approach sleep, Sabbath, or quiet time?
  12. If you truly believed that power belongs to God, what burden might you be able to put down?
  13. Which verse in Psalm 62 feels like an invitation to you right now?

ACTIVITY

Journal

Pour Out Your Heart

Spend several minutes writing honestly to God. Write about the things you are trying to control, prove, fix, or carry.

Then write a short prayer of surrender.

Always End Here

What is one small, concrete way you could practice rest in God this week?
